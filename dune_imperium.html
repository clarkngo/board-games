Dune: Imperium

Dune: Imperium blends deck building and worker placement for 1–4 players. As leaders of the Great Houses, you send agents across Arrakis and the Landsraad, win influence with the factions, and fight for control of the spice.

Objective

Be the first to reach 10 victory points, or have the most VP when the Conflict deck runs out. The game ends at the end of the round in which someone hits 10 VP (or after the final Conflict). Ties are broken by spice, then Solari, then water, then garrisoned troops.

Game Setup

  1. Board: Place the main board and put a bonus spice token supply nearby. Shuffle the Imperium deck and deal five cards face up to form the Imperium Row. Set out the Reserve piles (Arrakis Liaison, The Spice Must Flow, Foldspace).
  2. Conflict Deck: Build it from 1 Level I card, 5 Level II cards, and 4 Level III cards, with Level III on the bottom.
  3. Leaders: Each player picks a leader and takes its color's agents, troops, councilor, markers, and the 10-card starter deck.
  4. Starting Resources: Each player takes 1 water and puts 3 troops in their garrison. Place influence markers at 0 on each faction track and VP markers at 1.
  5. Intrigue: Shuffle the Intrigue deck. Draw five cards from your deck and choose a first player.

Round Structure

  1. Round Start: Reveal the next Conflict card and draw a hand of five cards.
  2. Player Turns: Clockwise, either take an Agent turn (play a card to send an agent to a matching board space) or a Reveal turn (reveal the rest of your hand for Persuasion and swords). Once you reveal, you are done for the round.
  3. Combat: Compare strength in the Conflict; rewards go to 1st, 2nd (and 3rd in 4-player).
  4. Makers: Add bonus spice to unoccupied maker spaces (Imperial Basin, Hagga Basin, The Great Flat).
  5. Recall: Return agents, discard your played cards and hand, and pass the First Player marker.

Key Concepts

  • Agent icons: A card's icons say which board areas it can send an agent to. Each space holds only one agent per round.
  • Persuasion: Revealed cards produce Persuasion to buy cards from the Imperium Row or Reserve into your discard pile.
  • Factions: Emperor, Spacing Guild, Bene Gesserit, and Fremen. Visiting their spaces raises influence. 2 influence = 1 VP; 4 influence = a bonus; the highest player at 4+ holds the Alliance token (+1 VP).
  • Combat spaces: When you place an agent on a combat-marked space, you may deploy up to 2 garrison troops plus any recruited this turn into the Conflict.
  • Intrigue cards: Hidden one-shot effects: plots on your turn, combat cards during combat, endgame cards at the end.
  • High Council & Mentat: Buy a council seat for +2 Persuasion every reveal; hire the Mentat for an extra agent this round.

Combat

Each troop is worth 2 strength and each sword revealed adds 1, but swords only count if you have at least one unit in the Conflict. Players may then play Intrigue combat cards in turn order. Rewards go by rank; ties share a lower reward. Units in the Conflict return to supply afterward.

Strategy Tips

  • Pick a lane early: Most winning plans lean on two factions. Spreading influence thinly gets no VP and no Alliances.
  • Trim and upgrade your deck: Every Persuasion card you buy makes the weak starters show up less. Cards with good agent icons and strong reveal effects are worth the most.
  • Get the High Council seat: The +2 Persuasion every round snowballs if you take it in the first few rounds.
  • Fight for the right conflicts: Commit troops when the reward is VP or you can win cheaply. Otherwise take 2nd place for spice, or sit it out and build your garrison.
  • Watch maker spice: Spice piles up on unvisited maker spaces. A big Harvest can fund a Council seat or a Mentat in one turn.
  • Timing your reveal: Revealing late shows less to opponents and gives you the final say in combat. Revealing early can deny strong Imperium Row cards.
  • Hold Intrigue for the swing: Combat Intrigue cards win close conflicts, and endgame cards can decide a tight race to 10.
  • Steal Alliances late: An Alliance token moves when someone passes you, which is a two-VP swing. Push past a leader's influence near the end.
