Caverna

Caverna: The Cave Farmers is a worker-placement game for 1–7 players. You lead a family of dwarves living in a mountain cave: clear the forest to farm and raise animals, dig into the mountain for rooms, mines, and rubies, and arm your dwarves to go on expeditions.

Objective

Have the most gold points after 12 rounds. Points come from animals, crops, furnishings, rubies, dwarves, pastures, mines, and gold, minus penalties for unused spaces, missing animal types, and begging.

Game Setup

  1. Boards: Each player takes a home board (forest on the left, mountain on the right) with a starting dwelling and two dwarves.
  2. Action Spaces: Set out the action boards for your player count. Shuffle the round cards by stage so a new action appears each round.
  3. Furnishings: Lay out the furnishing tiles (dwellings, workshops, and scoring rooms) where everyone can see them.
  4. Supply: Put goods, animals, and tiles in a common supply. Give starting food per the rulebook based on turn order.

Round Structure

  1. New Action: Reveal the next round card, adding a new action space.
  2. Replenish: Add goods to the accumulating action spaces.
  3. Work Phase: In turn order, place one dwarf at a time on an unoccupied action space and resolve it immediately. Unarmed dwarves go first, then armed ones from weakest to strongest weapon.
  4. Return Home: Bring all dwarves back.
  5. Harvest (most rounds): Harvest one grain or vegetable from each sown field, feed each dwarf 2 food (newborn offspring need 1), then each animal type with 2+ animals breeds one baby.

Key Concepts

  • Forest: Clear it into meadows and fields; fence pastures to hold animals. Dogs on meadows can guard sheep.
  • Mountain: Dig out caverns and tunnels, then furnish caverns with rooms. Deep tunnels and mines hold donkeys and rubies.
  • Family Growth: Needs a free dwelling slot. More dwarves mean more actions but more food at harvest.
  • Weapons & Expeditions: Forge a weapon from ore to send a dwarf on expeditions for loot. Its strength grows by 1 after each expedition.
  • Rubies: Wild resources, can be spent at any time for goods, animals, tiles, or to place a dwarf out of weapon order.
  • Food: Convert goods and animals to food at any time. If you can't feed, take a begging card (−3 points each).

Scoring

  • 1 point per farm animal and dog; −2 for each missing type among sheep, donkeys, wild boar, and cattle.
  • Grain: half your total, rounded up. Vegetables: 1 each. Rubies: 1 each. Dwarves: 1 each.
  • Points printed on furnishings, pastures, and mines, plus bonus points from scoring rooms and gold coins.
  • −1 per unused space on your home board, −3 per begging card.

Strategy Tips

  • Grow your family early: Extra dwarves compound over 12 rounds. Prioritize dwellings and Family Growth in the first half.
  • Set up food early: A cooking workshop, sown fields, or breeding animals give steady food. Begging cards are crippling at −3 each.
  • Pick a scoring plan: Scoring rooms (like the ones rewarding weapons, stone, or goods) decide your best path. Commit by mid-game.
  • Weapons pay off over time: An early expedition dwarf grows stronger each trip and can take loot in place of many other actions. Arm one dwarf early, not all of them.
  • Fill your board: Every unused space is −1 point. Plan to clear and dig steadily so you aren't scrambling in the last rounds.
  • Get every animal type: Missing sheep, donkeys, boar, or cattle costs 2 points each. One of each is an easy fix.
  • Use rubies for tempo: Rubies fill a missing resource, buy a tile, or let you place a dwarf out of turn order. Mines and ruby mines make them a renewable resource.
  • Watch accumulating spaces: Spaces that haven't been taken grow every round. A big pile of wood or food can be worth more than a new action.
